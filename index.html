Tuberculosis (TB) Treatment Guidelines (WHO Standard Updates)

Drug-Susceptible (DS-TB), Drug-Resistant (DR/MDR-TB), and Tuberculosis Preventive Treatment (TPT).

WHO Guidelines
1

Drug-Susceptible TB (DS-TB)

Standard 6-Month Regimen: • 2 Months Intensive: HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol).
• 4 Months Continuation: HR (Isoniazid, Rifampicin).
WHO Short 4-Month Regimen (Age ≥ 12 yrs): • Regimen: 2HPZMX / 2HPMX (High-Dose Rifapentine + INH + Pyrazinamide + Moxifloxacin).
• Indicated for eligible non-severe pulmonary DS-TB.
2

MDR/RR-TB (6-Month All-Oral BPaLM)

WHO Preferred BPaLM Regimen (6 Months): • Components: Bedaquiline + Pretomanid + Linezolid (600mg) + Moxifloxacin.
• BPaL: (Without Moxifloxacin) if fluoroquinolone resistance is confirmed.
Linezolid Toxicity Monitoring: Monitor weekly CBC for myelosuppression/anemia and inspect for peripheral neuropathy or optic neuritis.
3

TPT (Latent TB) & Pyridoxine Rules

3HP: Weekly Rifapentine + INH 3 Months
1HP: Daily Rifapentine + INH 1 Month
3HR: Daily INH + Rifampicin 3 Months
4R: Daily Rifampicin monotherapy 4 Months
Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) Co-prescription: Co-administer 25–50 mg daily with Isoniazid in pregnant/lactating women, HIV patients, alcohol use disorder, diabetes, or malnutrition to prevent peripheral neuropathy.

Syphilis Staging & Treatment Protocol (CDC & WHO)

Stage-based antimicrobial regimens, pregnancy rules, desensitization, and reaction management.

CDC / WHO Protocol

Primary, Secondary & Early Latent (<1 Year)

Early Stage
First-Line Therapy: Benzathine Penicillin G 2.4 Million Units IM in a single dose.
Non-Pregnant Penicillin Allergy Alternative: Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID for 14 days OR Ceftriaxone 1g IV/IM daily for 10–14 days.

Late Latent, Tertiary, or Unknown Duration

Late Stage
First-Line Therapy: Benzathine Penicillin G 2.4 Million Units IM weekly x 3 doses (Total 7.2 Million Units).
Non-Pregnant Penicillin Allergy Alternative: Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID for 28 days.

Neurosyphilis / Ocular / Otic Syphilis

CNS / Organ
First-Line Therapy: Aqueous Crystalline Penicillin G 18–24 Million Units IV daily (3–4 MU q4h or continuous infusion) for 10–14 days.
Alternative Option: Procaine Penicillin G 2.4 MU IM daily + Probenecid 500mg PO QID for 10-14 days.

Syphilis in Pregnancy (CRITICAL MANDATE)

Penicillin G is the ONLY proven effective therapy for preventing maternal transmission to the fetus and treating fetal infection.
Mandatory Action: Any pregnant woman with a penicillin allergy MUST be admitted for inpatient oral or IV Penicillin Desensitization followed immediately by Penicillin G administration. Doxycycline is strictly contraindicated.

Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction Counseling

Acute febrile reaction accompanied by headache, myalgia, tachycardia, and flushing occurring within 24 hours of initiating therapy (due to massive spirochete lysis).
Management: Symptomatic support with Antipyretics (Acetaminophen). Counsel pregnant patients that reaction may precipitate preterm labor or fetal distress; monitor accordingly.

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STDs / STIs) Guidelines

First-line regimens for Gonorrhea, Chlamydia, Trichomoniasis, Mycoplasma, and Pelvic Inflammatory Disease.

CDC / WHO Standard

Neisseria gonorrhoeae

Weight-Based First Line: • Weight < 150 kg: Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose.
• Weight ≥ 150 kg: Ceftriaxone 1 g IM single dose.
Chlamydial Co-infection Rule: If Chlamydia has not been excluded, add Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID x 7 days.

Chlamydia trachomatis

Preferred First Line: Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID for 7 days.
Alternative / Pregnancy: Azithromycin 1 g PO single dose OR Amoxicillin 500 mg PO TID x 7 days.

Trichomonas vaginalis

Females: Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID for 7 days (superior cure rate over single dose).
Males: Metronidazole 2 g PO single dose.

Mycoplasma genitalium

Two-Step Resistance-Guided Regimen: Step 1: Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID for 7 days.
Step 2: Follow immediately with Moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily for 7 days.

Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID Outpatient Triple Therapy)

1. Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose
2. Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID x 14 days
3. Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID x 14 days

Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4) Clinical Protocol Engine

Severe Pre-eclampsia / Eclampsia management and Fetal Neuroprotection for preterm delivery.

Obstetric Protocol

Severe Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia Regimens

Zuspan Regimen (IV Preferred): • Loading Dose: 4g - 6g IV in 100 mL IV fluid over 15–20 minutes.
• Maintenance: 1g - 2g / hour continuous IV infusion for 24 hours postpartum or post-last seizure.
Pritchard Regimen (IM Alternative): • Loading Dose: 4g IV + 10g IM (5g in each buttock).
• Maintenance: 5g IM into alternating buttocks every 4 hours.

Mandatory Safety Monitoring & Antidote

Check Hourly Before Every Maintenance Dose:

• Patellar Tendon Reflex: Must be PRESENT (lost at 9.6–12 mg/dL).

• Respiratory Rate: Must be > 12–16 breaths/min (respiratory depression at >12 mg/dL).

• Urine Output: Must be > 30 mL/hour (renal excretion of Mg).

Magnesium Toxicity Antidote: CALCIUM GLUCONATE 1 Gram IV Slow Push (10 mL of 10% solution administered IV over 3–5 minutes).

Fetal Neuroprotection Protocol (Preterm Delivery < 32 Weeks)

Clinical Indication: Imminent preterm birth at < 32 weeks gestation to reduce the risk of Cerebral Palsy and gross motor dysfunction.
Dosing Regimen: • Loading Dose: 4g IV over 20–30 minutes.
• Maintenance: 1g / hour IV until delivery or for up to 24 hours.
Retreatment Rule: If delivery does not occur within 24 hours and is again imminent later, a repeat 4g loading dose may be considered.

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) Guidelines (ACOG / WHO / ADA)

Diagnostic criteria, glycemic targets, and stepwise medical nutrition to pharmacotherapy.

ACOG / WHO Standard

Diagnostic Criteria

1-Step 75g OGTT (IADPSG / WHO): Diagnostic if ANY ONE value is met/exceeded:

• Fasting: ≥ 92 mg/dL (5.1 mmol/L)

• 1-Hour: ≥ 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L)

• 2-Hour: ≥ 153 mg/dL (8.5 mmol/L)

2-Step Approach (ACOG): 50g non-fasting screen (≥135-140 mg/dL) followed by 100g 3-hr OGTT (2 or more abnormal thresholds).

Pregnancy Glycemic Targets

Fasting Blood Glucose: < 95 mg/dL
1-Hour Postprandial: < 140 mg/dL
2-Hour Postprandial: < 120 mg/dL

Stepwise Management

Step 1: Lifestyle & MNT Medical Nutrition Therapy + postprandial walking for 1–2 weeks.
Step 2: First-Line Pharmacotherapy INSULIN is the First-Line Standard of Care (NPH, Regular, Detemir, Aspart/Lispro) as it does not cross the placenta.
Step 3: Oral Alternatives Metformin or Glyburide used only if patient refuses or cannot safely administer insulin (crosses placenta).
